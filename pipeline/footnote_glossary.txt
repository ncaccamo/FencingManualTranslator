# Footnote glossary for hard-to-translate fencing terms.
#
# 2_translate.py footnotes the FIRST occurrence per page of any term below
# that the translation kept in French as <i>term</i>. Format:
#
#   term | gloss
#
# `term` is matched case-insensitively against the text inside <i>...</i>
# (accents ignored). Keep the gloss to one clause. Lines starting with #
# and blank lines are ignored. Order does not matter.

liement        | continuous blade contact that carries the opponent's steel aside without losing touch
froissement    | a sharp grazing shock along the opponent's blade, from forte to foible, to knock it out of line
coulé          | a thrust that glides along the opponent's blade while keeping contact
prise de fer   | any action that seizes and controls the opponent's blade before attacking (bind, cross, envelopment, press)
croisé         | a prise de fer that carries the opponent's blade diagonally from a high line to a low line, or the reverse
doublé         | an attack that deceives the opponent's counter-parry by describing a full circle around it
dégagement     | a simple attack that passes the point under or over the opponent's blade to the opposite line
coupé          | a simple attack that passes the point over the opponent's tip rather than under the blade
appel          | a sharp stamp of the front foot, used to provoke a reaction or mark tempo
fente          | the lunge — the driving extension onto the front leg that carries the point to the target
remise         | replacing the point in the same line, without withdrawing the arm, after the first thrust missed
reprise        | a renewed attack made after returning to guard, distinct from a remise
temps d'arrêt  | a stop-thrust: a counter-hit landed into the opponent's attack, in the tempo of its preparation
sentiment du fer | the trained tactile perception of pressure and intention through blade contact
à-propos       | the judgement of the right moment to act — timing married to opportunity
